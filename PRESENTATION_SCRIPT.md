## SLIDE 3 — INTRODUCTION

"Now, let's talk about what paper and pulp actually is.

**Paper** is a product manufactured from wood that has been reduced into individual fibers — called pulp — which are then formed into a mat from a slurry with water, compressed, and dried.

**Pulp**, on the other hand, is a fibrous material derived from wood or non-wood sources such as cotton, sugarcane bagasse, and rice straw — processed either chemically or mechanically.

Paper comes in many grades — from printing and writing paper, newsprint, corrugating containerboards, tissue and sanitary papers, to specialty boards like art paper.

Now here is a critical industry context in the Philippines: **The only integrated pulp and paper mill in the country — PICOP Resources Inc. — ceased operations in 2010**, due to lack of investment capital and inability to sustain its tree plantation program.

Because of this, the Philippine paper industry now **primarily relies on recycled wastepaper** as its main raw material. In fact, wastepaper accounts for 19% of total municipal solid waste in the Philippines — representing a significant and largely untapped resource.

To supplement quality and quantity, mills also import virgin pulp and wastepaper from abroad."

## SLIDE 6 — RAW MATERIALS

"Let us now discuss the raw materials used in paper and pulp production.

There are three main categories:

**First — Virgin or Wood Pulp.** This includes softwood pulp from pine and spruce trees, which has long fibers ideal for strong printing and writing paper; and hardwood pulp from eucalyptus and birch, which has shorter fibers that improve paper bulk and opacity.

**Second — Non-Wood Pulp.** These are sustainable alternatives from plants like abaca, banana, rice straw, wheat straw, bagasse, and bamboo. Among these, **abaca pulp** is especially notable — it produces exceptionally strong specialty paper and is a key Philippine export commodity.

**Third — Recycled Wastepaper**, which is the PRIMARY raw material for our plant and for the Philippine industry as a whole. Used paper is processed through deinking and fiber separation. This approach offers major environmental advantages: it reduces waste, conserves natural resources, and decreases greenhouse gas emissions.

While local wastepaper is affordable, its quality and quantity often fall short — so our plant will supplement with imported virgin pulp and wastepaper when necessary."

## SLIDE 7 — CHEMICALS AND ADDITIVES

"Paper and pulp production requires a range of chemicals throughout the process.

For **pulping**, the two most critical chemicals are:
- **Caustic Soda or Sodium Hydroxide** — which breaks down wood chips into pulp, and
- **Sodium Sulfide** — which acts as a cooking liquor in the Kraft process, helping dissolve lignin from cellulose fibers.

For **bleaching**, we use:
- **Chlorine Dioxide** for high-level whiteness,
- **Hydrogen Peroxide** as an eco-friendly brightening agent,
- **Sodium Hypochlorite** for wastepaper pulp brightness improvement, and
- **Sodium Hydrosulfite** for mechanical pulp brightening.

And during **papermaking**, additives are introduced to improve the final product:
- **Fillers** like clay and calcium carbonate improve opacity, brightness, and printability;
- **Sizing agents** prevent ink feathering and add water resistance;
- **Retention aids, strengthening agents, slime control chemicals, antifoaming agents, and dyes** are added as process conditions require."

## SLIDE 8 — DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

"Now, let me discuss the **five key constraints** that guided the design of this plant.

**Constraint 1 — Environmental and Regulatory.** This is often the strictest constraint. The plant must manage effluent water quality — strictly controlling BOD, COD, pH, and total suspended solids. Air emissions — particularly sulfur compounds and particulate matter — must be controlled through advanced scrubbers and electrostatic precipitators. Solid waste like sludge, bark, and ash must be safely utilized or landfilled.

**Constraint 2 — Raw Material and Fiber.** The choice of raw material dictates the entire mill layout. Since we rely heavily on recycled fiber, the design incorporates intensive deinking, cleaning, and contaminant-removal systems. We also need large storage areas to ensure continuous supply.

**Constraint 3 — Energy and Utility.** Paper mills are highly energy-intensive. The design targets **co-generation or CHP** — using the recovery boiler burning black liquor to generate both steam and electricity — making the plant self-sufficient in energy.

**Constraint 4 — Technical and Process.** The plant employs Kraft chemical pulping and must use ECF or TCF bleaching sequences — avoiding toxic chlorinated compounds. Equipment in contact with harsh chemicals must use duplex stainless steel or titanium to resist corrosion.

**Constraint 5 — Site and Geographical.** The plant must be near a water source for intake and discharge. Proximity to ports and highways is essential for daily movement of raw materials and finished paper rolls."

## SLIDE 9 — PROCESS FLOW DIAGRAM

"Let me now walk you through the overall process flow of the plant.

The manufacturing journey begins with **Raw Material Preparation** — debarking the logs, cutting them to size, chipping them into uniform pieces, screening out oversized or undersized chips, and storing them in silos.

From there, the chips enter one of three **Pulping methods** — which I will explain in detail in the next slide.

After pulping, the fibers go through **Washing, Screening, and Cleaning** to remove impurities, then **Bleaching** to achieve the desired brightness.

The prepared pulp then enters **Stock Preparation** — where it is beaten, blended, and mixed with fillers, sizing agents, and other chemicals.

Finally, the pulp proceeds to the **Papermaking Stage** — from the headbox, through the wire section, press section, dryer section, size press, calendar, and finally wound into finished rolls at the reel and rewinder."

---

## SLIDE 10 — PULPING METHODS

"Our plant utilizes three pulping methods, depending on the raw material.

**Method 1 — Chemical Pulping, specifically the Kraft Process.** Wood chips and white liquor — a mixture of sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide — are loaded into a digester at approximately 160 degrees Celsius and 10 atmospheres of pressure. This cooks the chips, separating cellulose fibers from lignin. The fibers are then washed and sent for bleaching. The remaining liquid — called black liquor — goes to the chemical recovery process, where it is burned in recovery boilers to generate energy and recover chemicals for reuse.

**Method 2 — Mechanical Pulping.** Here, wood chips are physically broken down using a refiner equipped with two rotating disks. The fibers are then screened, bleached, and thickened. This method retains most of the lignin, producing a higher yield but lower-strength pulp.

**Method 3 — Wastepaper Pulping.** Collected wastepaper is loaded into a hydrapulper with water and agitated at high speed for 30 to 60 minutes to re-slurry the fibers. Foreign objects are removed using screens and cleaners. The pulp then goes through deinking — where ink is chemically and mechanically separated from the fibers — followed by bleaching to achieve the required brightness of 55 to 75 percent."

---

## SLIDE 11 — STOCK PREPARATION

"After pulping and bleaching, the pulp enters the **Stock Preparation** stage before it can be made into paper.

There are three key steps here:

First — **Beating.** The pulp is passed through a disk refiner, which swells the fibers, increases their flexibility and surface area, and improves their ability to bond with each other — which directly affects paper strength and sheet formation quality.

Second — **Blending.** Raw materials are blended together, including recycled broke — which is paper trim or defective paper from the production process. This ensures nothing goes to waste.

Third — **Addition of Fillers, Sizing Agents, and Chemicals.** Fillers like clay or calcium carbonate are mixed in to improve opacity and brightness. Sizing agents are added to control how the paper absorbs ink and water. And various performance chemicals — retention aids, strengthening agents, slime control agents, antifoaming agents, and dyes — are added as needed."

---

## SLIDE 12 — PAPERMAKING STAGE

"We now reach the final and most critical stage — **Papermaking.**

The process begins at the **Headbox**, which distributes the fiber-water suspension uniformly across the full width of the paper machine wire.

On the **Wire Section**, the fibers settle into a mat while water drains away — forming the initial paper sheet.

The sheet then moves into the **Press Section**, where it passes through rollers under high pressure with an absorbent felt cloth, mechanically removing more water while increasing paper strength and surface quality.

After the press, the wet paper enters the **Dryer Section** — a series of steam-heated cylinders that reduce moisture content to 6 to 10 percent.

The **Size Press** then applies starch to the paper surface, giving it water resistance and surface strength.

The paper passes through a **Calendar** — a stack of steel rollers — which smooths the surface, adds gloss, and ensures uniform thickness.

Finally, the paper is wound into large jumbo rolls at the **Pope Reel** and then cut and trimmed to sellable widths at the **Rewinder** before being shipped as finished product."

## SLIDE 16 — DESIGN COMPUTATIONS

"Let me briefly walk through the key design computations.

For a target production of **1,200 kilograms of paper per hour**, the mass balance shows:
- We need approximately **1,333 kilograms of dry fiber** — accounting for process losses,
- About **25,333 kilograms of water** — reflecting the approximately 1% consistency of the papermaking slurry,
- For a total input of around **26,667 kilograms of pulp slurry per hour.**
- The dryer section must evaporate approximately **1,800 kilograms of water per hour** to bring the paper to finished moisture content.

For piping material selection — the choice of pipe material depends on the type of pulp being conveyed. Kraft and sulfite pulps use **copper pipe**. Mechanical and groundwood pulps — which are highly abrasive — use **PVC pipe**. Long-fiber kraft pulps use **stainless steel or PVC** depending on whether they are dried or never-dried. Soda pulps use **steel pipe.**

This careful material selection ensures long service life, corrosion resistance, and minimal contamination of the pulp."

## POSSIBLE Q&A ANSWERS

**Q: Bakit hindi na lang sa Mindanao itayo ang planta kung mas maroon raw materials doon?**

A: "Although Mindanao has more indigenous fiber sources like tree plantations and agricultural waste, locating the plant there would result in higher skilled labor costs, greater plant investment costs, higher freight costs for distributing finished products, and longer delivery times to the major market center which is Metro Manila. SJDM's proximity to both raw material sources and market distribution channels makes it a more cost-effective and strategically sound location."

---

**Q: Bakit recycled wastepaper ang primary raw material at hindi virgin wood pulp?**

A: "The Philippines no longer has an integrated pulp mill operating domestically since PICOP shut down in 2010. There is simply no local virgin wood pulp source available at scale. Recycled wastepaper, on the other hand, is abundantly available in Metro Manila and urbanized areas, is more affordable, and is environmentally advantageous — reducing waste, conserving resources, and lowering greenhouse gas emissions. Virgin pulp is only imported to supplement quality when required."

---

**Q: Ano ang Kraft Process at bakit ito ang piniling chemical pulping method?**

A: "The Kraft Process uses a combination of sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide — called white liquor — to cook wood chips at high temperature and pressure. It is chosen because it produces the strongest pulp among all chemical pulping methods, it is highly adaptable to any wood species, and critically — the chemicals can be recovered and recycled through the recovery boiler system, making it both economical and environmentally efficient."

---

**Q: Paano ninyong maiiwasan ang environmental impact ng planta?**

A: "The plant design incorporates several environmental safeguards. For water — we use closed-loop water systems and effluent treatment plants to control BOD, COD, TSS, and pH before any discharge. For air — we use advanced recovery boilers, scrubbers, and electrostatic precipitators to control sulfur compounds and particulate matter. For solid waste — bark is burned for fuel in the biomass boiler, ash and lime mud are recycled back into the process, and residual sludge is handled through secure landfilling. The co-generation system also reduces our external energy demand significantly."

---

**Q: Ilan ang manpower at bakit 80 lang?**

A: "The 80-person workforce is designed for an efficient, modern paper mill operation. Modern paper mills are highly automated — especially the papermaking machine itself — which reduces the need for large manual labor forces. The 80 personnel cover all critical functions: production, quality control, maintenance, logistics, administration, safety, and management. This lean but complete structure ensures operational efficiency while remaining financially sustainable for a mid-capacity plant."
